As an undergraduate research assistant at Northern Kentucky University, I worked with complex ecological datasets using spatial and statistical methods.

Northern Kentucky University is a public university in Highland Heights, in the Cincinnati metropolitan area. Its Department of Biological Sciences offers undergraduate research opportunities alongside its degree tracks. In this role I handled the data side of ecological research: organizing field and survey data, then using GIS and statistics to answer the project's questions.

- Collected, organized, and analyzed ecological datasets.
- Applied spatial and statistical methods to research questions.
- Contributed to conservation-focused ecological research.

My main project here was a study of Culex mosquito egg-laying, funded by the [Dr. Carol Swarts Milburn Research Fellowship](/awards/milburn-research-fellowship/). It earned [1st place in the Entomological Society of America's undergraduate poster competition](/awards/poster-competition-1st-place/) and was published in PLOS ONE as [The Goldilocks principle](/publications/weidig-goldilocks-principle-22/). The role ran alongside my [B.S. in Biological Sciences](/education/nku-bachelors/), where I was named [Outstanding Graduate in Biology](/awards/outstanding-graduate-biology/).
