I built the website for the Eastern Fire Network (EFNet), a research network studying wildfire risks, impacts, and preparedness across the eastern United States. EFNet brings together researchers, land managers, and decision-makers to set a shared research agenda, find data gaps, and inform management and policy.

The site is built in Google Sites so the team can keep editing it without code. It covers the network's mission, the team, research, and resources, with a contact page.

EFNet launched in late 2025 and is led by Erica Smithwick (Penn State). Partner institutions include Penn State, NC State, and the Cary Institute of Ecosystem Studies, and the network is funded through the NSF's Fire Science Innovations through Research and Education (FIRE) program, with support from the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation and the Buckminster Fuller Institute. Fire conditions in the East are changing quickly, and eastern wildfire is closely tied to development: the wildland-urban interface covers more of the East than the West, which is why the network includes work on predictive modeling, community vulnerability, and smoke exposure and health.

Annual workshops bring together researchers, land managers, and other stakeholders, and the network runs a graduate research experience in transdisciplinary eastern wildland fire science, where students work in interdisciplinary teams with practitioners on local fire science challenges.
