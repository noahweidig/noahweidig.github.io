I completed my B.S. in Biological Sciences at Northern Kentucky University on the Ecology, Evolution, & Organismal track, graduating with a 4.0 GPA.

Northern Kentucky University is a public university in Highland Heights, Kentucky, just outside Cincinnati. Its biological sciences degree offers several tracks; the Ecology, Evolution, & Organismal track covers how organisms interact with their environments and how populations and ecosystems change over time, with a foundation in chemistry, physics, and mathematics.

During my time at NKU I worked as a [research assistant](/experience/nku-research/) on ecological data projects and was recognized as the [Outstanding Graduate in Biology](/awards/outstanding-graduate-biology/) by the Department of Biological Sciences. My research on mosquito egg-laying was funded by the [Dr. Carol Swarts Milburn Research Fellowship](/awards/milburn-research-fellowship/), won [1st place at the ESA student poster competition](/awards/poster-competition-1st-place/), and was published in PLOS ONE as [The Goldilocks principle](/publications/weidig-goldilocks-principle-22/). I stayed on the [President's List](/awards/presidents-list/) all four years.
